Field guidance

Responsible marine mammal watching

International best-practice standards, adapted for the Maldives — so every encounter stays safe and respectful for cetaceans and people alike.

Why this matters

Watching with care

The Maldives is home to a remarkable diversity of dolphins and whales, offering some of the world's most memorable marine wildlife encounters.

Currently, there are no national guidelines governing dolphin and whale watching in the Maldives. While most encounters are conducted responsibly, increasing vessel traffic has the potential to impact cetaceans through disturbance, reduced resting opportunities, and vessel strikes.

These recommendations have been developed by Koamas — drawing on international best-practice standards and adapted for Maldivian waters — to promote safe, respectful, and sustainable encounters for both marine mammals and people.

For all encounters

General guidelines

Let the animals choose

Allow dolphins and whales to approach your vessel on their own terms. Never chase, pursue, or pressure animals into an interaction.

Slow down

Reduce speed when marine mammals are nearby, and hold a steady, predictable course.

Never block their path

Always leave a clear, open route so animals can move freely.

Give mothers & calves extra space

Avoid close approaches, and never position your vessel between a mother and her calf.

Wildlife comes first

Photographs and close encounters should never come at the expense of animal welfare.

Support conservation

Share your sightings and photographs with Koamas to help us understand marine mammals in the Maldives.

Extra care

Additional considerations for whales

Whales are particularly vulnerable to vessel disturbance and collisions, due to their size, their behaviour, and the time they spend at the surface.

Approach with caution

Slow down and give whales ample space to continue their natural behaviour.

Do not pursue

If a whale changes direction, dives repeatedly, or moves away, do not follow.

Take extra care around mothers & calves

Increase your viewing distance and minimise vessel movement around mothers and young calves.

Reduce vessel strike risk

Maintain a slow speed and keep a careful lookout whenever whales are present.

At a glance

Recommended distances

Recommended minimum distances and limits when watching cetaceans, based on international best practice. Always give animals more room if they show any sign of disturbance.

Whales
Keep at least 100 m away; do not approach head-on or from directly behind.
Dolphins
Keep at least 50 m away; let them set the distance.
Caution zone
Within 100–300 m, slow to a no-wake speed and hold a steady course.
Vessels present
No more than 2 boats with a group at once; others wait their turn or move on.
Time with a group
Limit close viewing to about 20–30 minutes, then move away.

The Koamas Golden Rule

If the animal chooses to approach, enjoy the encounter. If your presence causes it to change its behaviour, give it more space.
