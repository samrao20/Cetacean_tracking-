About Koamas

Koamas — the citizen-science project behind Maldives Cetacean Tracker

About the project

Koamas was founded in the Maldives by James, Samay, and Jacob, brought together by a shared fascination with cetaceans and a deep respect for the ocean that connects these islands.

The idea began in response to a simple gap: dolphin sightings in the Maldives are frequent, but the information is often fragmented, unrecorded, or lost after a single encounter. Koamas was created to change that — to bring structure, continuity, and meaning to these moments at sea.

By combining citizen observations with structured identification and long-term data collection, Koamas aims to build a clearer understanding of the dolphins that inhabit Maldivian waters, their movements, and their behaviours across atolls.

At its core, Koamas is both a research tool and a shared space for anyone who spends time on the water — from local communities to marine professionals and visitors — to contribute to something larger than a single sighting.

This is an ongoing project built with curiosity, collaboration, and care for the marine life that defines the Maldives.

Meet the team

Koamas was founded by James, Samay, and Jacob — three people brought together by a shared connection to the ocean and a goal of improving how dolphin sightings are recorded and understood in the Maldives. The initial concept was developed by James, building on his cetacean research and fieldwork in the region.

J

James

Cetacean Marine Biologist & Founder

James is a cetacean marine biologist and professional underwater photographer based in the Maldives. He has field experience across the Great Barrier Reef, including marine research and the Crown-of-Thorns Starfish program. In the Maldives, he has been closely involved in the Dolphin ID Project, with a focus on cetacean research, fieldwork, and marine conservation.

S

Samay

Technical Lead

Samay is originally from Mumbai and completed a Master's degree at James Cook University. His interest in cetaceans developed during his professional placement in the Maldives, where he first worked on dolphin research. He leads the technical development of Koamas, focusing on how sightings data is structured, processed, and used for long-term analysis.

J

Jacob

Data & Research

Jacob is from the United States and also completed his Master's at James Cook University. He supports Koamas through data management and organisation, helping ensure sightings records are accurately maintained and usable for research. His wider focus is on coral reef ecosystems and how habitat health connects to marine biodiversity, including cetaceans.

Koamas grows with every sighting shared. If you spend time on the water in the Maldives, your observations matter.
