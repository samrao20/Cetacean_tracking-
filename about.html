About the project

A citizen science initiative building the first long-term record of cetacean sightings across the Maldivian archipelago.

What does Koamas mean?

Koamas (ކޯމަސް) is the Dhivehi word for dolphin. Dhivehi is the official language of the Maldives, spoken by the approximately 500,000 people who call this Indian Ocean archipelago home.

We chose the name because it roots the project firmly in the Maldivian community. These waters and the animals in them have been part of island life for centuries — long before modern science arrived.

Dolphins are among the most frequently spotted cetaceans in the atolls. For many Maldivians and visitors alike, a koamas encounter is a defining memory of the islands.

The idea

How Koamas started

Dolphins and whales are seen constantly around the Maldives — from dive boats, ferries, liveaboards, and resort jetties. Almost none of those encounters were ever written down.

That gap is what Koamas set out to close: not a research survey run for a few weeks a year, but a standing, always-open channel that anyone on the water can use the moment they see something.

Koamas is founded by Samay and James, working in collaboration with Reefscapers and Marine Savers to turn a shared interest in cetaceans into a structured, lasting citizen science project.

The project

What we do

Koamas is a citizen science platform that collects, verifies, and publishes cetacean sighting data from across the Maldivian archipelago. Anyone who sees a dolphin or whale can report it through WhatsApp or the web form.

Our team reviews each report, confirms the species identification where possible, and adds verified sightings to the public record.

The Maldives sits at the heart of the Indian Ocean, and its waters are home to at least 23 species of dolphins and whales.

Science

Methodology

Sightings are submitted through WhatsApp or the web form. Reports capture species, approximate location, date and time, group size, and an optional photo.

Our verification team checks each submission against field guide criteria, photo evidence where available, and known distribution data.

Privacy

Privacy & data

Reporter identity is protected. Personal identifiers are not displayed on the public site, and sighting positions may be delayed or approximate.

Sighting data is published openly once verified. Photos are not published without permission.
